# Narration craft: making the story gripping (learned 2026-11-19)

Sources: Trey Parker & Matt Stone's "but / therefore" rule (NYU talk); documentary narration
guides (StudioBinder, Ken Burns MasterClass: "history is detective work"); Shorts retention
guides (OpusClip, ShortsFast, Stratboost, UpTube). Their tips, turned into rules for our Hindi
mystery Shorts and long videos.

## The 8 rules

1. **Hook = the strangest true fact, in under 3 seconds.** Open mid-action or on the
   impossible thing itself, not on the place's name or a greeting. Never a claim the video
   can't back up: the payoff must match the promise.
   - weak: "आज हम बात करेंगे गुजरात के एक मंदिर की।"
   - strong: "ये शिवलिंग... दिन में दो बार समुद्र में डूब जाता है।"

2. **Open a question early, answer it late (the open loop).** Show, then tell: let the viewer
   wonder "कैसे? क्यों?" for most of the video, and give the answer (or the best answer) near
   the end. Say the question out loud once ("पर ऐसा होता क्यों है?").

3. **But / therefore, never "and then".** Each scene must follow from the last ("इसलिए", "तो")
   or turn it ("पर", "लेकिन", "मगर"). A list of facts joined by "और फिर" is a slideshow, not a
   story. Check every link between scenes before finishing.

4. **Re-hook every 2–3 scenes.** A turn that resets attention: "पर असली रहस्य ये नहीं है...",
   "और यहीं से कहानी बदल जाती है...", "लेकिन एक बात किसी को समझ नहीं आई..." Use each phrase
   at most once per video.

5. **Specific beats general.** A time, a number, a sound, a texture: "दोपहर होते-होते पानी
   घुटनों तक", not "फिर पानी आता है". Only details the sources support (no invented numbers).

6. **Tell it as a discovery (limited knowledge).** Reveal things in the order a visitor or
   investigator would find them out. Hold back what they didn't know yet.

7. **Say what the picture can't.** The image shows the temple; the line adds the feeling, the
   stakes or the fact behind it. Don't narrate the obvious ("ये एक मंदिर है").

8. **End on a payoff that echoes the hook** (for Shorts, ideally so it loops: the last line
   leads back into the first). Belief as belief, fact as fact; one closing line, no CTA.

## Hindi voice style

- Talk to the viewer: "सोचिए...", "आप वहाँ खड़े हैं...", rhetorical questions.
- Short spoken sentences; "..." where a storyteller would pause for effect.
- Everyday words over bookish ones (ज्वार ok; avoid heavy Sanskrit-only words).
- Present tense for the gripping moments ("पानी चढ़ता है"), past tense for history.
- Emotion words sparingly and only where earned (डर, हैरानी, सन्नाटा).

## Self-check before validating a script

- [ ] Line 1 is the strangest true fact; no greeting, no place-name opener
- [ ] A question is planted by scene 3 and paid off near the end
- [ ] Every scene link is "but" or "therefore", not "and then"
- [ ] A re-hook every 2–3 scenes
- [ ] At least 3 specific, sourced details
- [ ] Last line echoes the first
